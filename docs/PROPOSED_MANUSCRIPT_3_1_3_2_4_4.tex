\paragraph{Category closure and admissibility witnesses.}
In $\mathbf{CAn}$, arrows are identified extensionally by their
underlying analytic maps. A Type--2 realizer, a certified Lipschitz
constant, and a finite-code compiler are witnesses of admissibility,
rather than additional components of arrow identity. We require the
finite-evidence grammar to supply reflexive zero-defect evidence for
an unchanged code and typed constructors for Lipschitz transport
and triangle composition of finite witnesses.

The identity is realized on names by the identity and on finite
codes by $p\mapsto p$ with its reflexive witness. For composable
maps $T:\mathfrak X\to\mathfrak Y$ and
$S:\mathfrak Y\to\mathfrak Z$, fix a nonnegative rational
Lipschitz bound $\Lambda_S$. At a requested rational accuracy
$\varepsilon>0$, run the code compiler of $T$ at
$\eta_T=\varepsilon/[3(1+\Lambda_S)]$ and that of $S$ on the
resulting code at $\eta_S=\varepsilon/3$. The typed Lipschitz and
triangle rules transport these finite witnesses to a witness for
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 d_Z(ST\rho_X(p),\rho_Z(r))
 &\leq\Lambda_S d_Y(T\rho_X(p),\rho_Y(q))
      +d_Z(S\rho_Y(q),\rho_Z(r))\\
 &\leq\Lambda_S\eta_T+\eta_S<\varepsilon.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The composite Type--2 realizer is $S^\sharp\circ T^\sharp$;
a composite Lipschitz constant is $\Lambda_S\Lambda_T$.
Associativity and unit laws hold extensionally at the level of
analytic maps. In $\mathbf{CAn}^{\uparrow}$, chosen evidence lifts
are genuinely part of arrow data; identity and composition use the
corresponding identity and composite lift functors. The stronger
evidence-regularity requirement of Definition~4.5 is not assumed
for arbitrary objects of $\mathbf{CAn}$.

\paragraph{Uniform input convention for Lemma~3.1.}
The vector $v$ is supplied through its Type--2 fast Cauchy name
$\nu_v$ in the specified computable Banach presentation. The
uniform extension procedure receives the presentation index,
$\nu_v$, and the rational tolerance $\eta>0$, with the promise
$\|\delta_X(\nu_v)\|>0$. No positive rational lower bound on
$\|v\|$ is required as separate input: from the name, the computable
core norms, and the fast-name tail estimates one computes rational
approximations $a_n$ with
$|a_n-\|v\||\leq 2^{1-n}$ and searches for
$a_n>2^{1-n}$. The search terminates on the promised domain,
and $a_n-2^{1-n}>0$ is an explicit rational lower bound on
$\|v\|$. The output of the approximate Hahn--Banach procedure is
a Type--2 name of a linear functional $g$ satisfying
$g(v)=\|v\|$ and $\|g\|\leq 1+\eta$.
The constructive \emph{extension} itself requires the separate
epsilon-Hahn--Banach algorithm and is not supplied merely by this
nonzero-norm preprocessing.

\paragraph{Effective finite nets for the dual compactum.}
The coordinate constraints describe the dual unit ball $K_X$
as a co-c.e.-closed subset of a computably compact Hilbert cube
$H$, furnishing negative information. Positive information is
constructed independently: the rational absolutely convex hull
of the computable norming family is weak-star dense in $K_X$
by the bipolar argument. Enumerate this dense family as
$d_0,d_1,\ldots$. For each prescribed dyadic $\delta>0$,
dovetail over finite prefixes $D_m=\{d_0,\ldots,d_m\}$ and
the ambient finite-open-cover semidecision
\[
 H\subseteq (H\setminus K_X)
   \cup\bigcup_{d\in D_m} B_H(d,\delta).
\]
The complement $H\setminus K_X$ is c.e. open. Ordinary compactness
and density guarantee that some prefix passes the cover test;
computable compactness of $H$ supplies the effective positive
termination test. The resulting finite $\delta$-nets provide
the compact representation required for the effective Cantor
surjection. The evaluation embedding is separately proved
linear and isometric, and the Cantor-gap affine extension must
be both computable and linear. Computable metric universality
alone is not an adequate substitute.
